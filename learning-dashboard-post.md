Learning that actually sticks, end to end — on Muse.

Sharing the free guide 👇

1/ Start with your field, not a feed
Field, level, topics, and session length first. Three to five
topics is a good starting rotation, and you can add more later.
No feed, no endless scrolling, no course you have to binge.

2/ One real article at a time
A real, verified article, with its full link and source name.
No invented links. No made-up summaries. And never your private
notes dressed up as an article.

3/ Learn it properly, inside the dashboard
Summary of what it is about, what to remember, and a worked
example so you can see it used practically. The article opens
inside the dashboard, not in another browser tab you will lose.

4/ Practise it, don't just read it
A required quiz and exercise on every lesson. The quiz is a
self-check with a tick-box, not auto-graded. The exercise is
hands-on, in your field.

5/ Take notes you can trace back
Notes are filed by topic, all in one place. When you save a
note, its article is attached automatically as Proof — a linked
title with its source. Months later, you still know where an
idea came from. Edit or Delete any note, and Save note is the
button that saves it.

6/ Ask when you are stuck
Select any text in an article and ask for an explanation in
Explain This, or add it as a note. Explanations are saved with
the lesson. If you start a note and switch away without saving
— even to Explain This — your draft is saved and restored
exactly when you come back.

7/ Keep going, at your pace
Add a new topic in Settings and it joins the rotation. Past
and planned lessons live in Article Logs. You choose when to
learn, for 15 to 90 minutes. Topics you choose drive what comes
next — no difficulty prediction, no auto-generated future list.
