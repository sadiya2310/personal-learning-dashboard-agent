Using AI for UX Work: Study Guide

Thursday 12 November 2026 · Topic: AI / UX Workflows · Source: Nielsen Norman Group · 30 minutes

Read the full article on nngroup.com →

Summary

This Nielsen Norman Group study guide explains when and how to use AI across UX work — design, research, writing, service design, and ideation. The core idea: treat AI as a talented intern. You are the strategist; AI does supervised work inside the boundaries you set, and you check everything before it goes anywhere.

What to remember

  • Use AI when the task is well-defined and checkable; use your own judgment when it is ambiguous or high-stakes.
  • Write prompts with CARE: Context, Ask, Rules, Examples.
  • Synthetic users cannot replace real users — they are a rehearsal tool, not evidence.
  • Every AI output needs a human check before it is shared.

Worked example

Meet Ari, your AI UX intern. You ask Ari to draft interview questions for a checkout study. You give it context (a B2C checkout flow, first-time buyers), the ask (10 open-ended questions), the rules (no leading questions, plain language), and two example questions you like. Ari drafts ten questions in a minute. You then read them, cut three that lead the witness, rewrite two, and keep five. The intern did the drafting; you did the judging.

Quiz & Exercise

Self-check — tick each one when you can answer it.

Exercise. Pick a familiar task from any field and state which field you chose. Write one CARE prompt for it, map where AI assists and where a human owns the call, and write one check you would run on the AI's output before sharing it.

Sample question: "What does CARE mean in a prompt?"

Answer: Context (what the task is about), Ask (what you want), Rules (constraints to follow), Examples (samples of good output). Giving all four turns a vague request into a brief an AI can actually follow — and makes it easier for you to spot where it went off brief.
